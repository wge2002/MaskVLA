Abstract

Reducing trajectory overfitting with view-balanced training.

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models integrate vision-language understanding with executable robot actions, enabling end-to-end learning for robot control. However, when fine-tuned on limited manipulation datasets, existing models can overfit to demonstration trajectories and rely too heavily on the primary camera view.

MaskVLA introduces a simple masking-based fine-tuning strategy: randomly mask a small portion of the primary camera input during training so the policy learns to use wrist-camera information more effectively. On RoboTwin2.0, MaskVLA improves average success by 23.2% over π0 and 16.8% over OpenVLA-OFT, with additional gains on real-world ALOHA tasks.

Method

Random masking on the primary camera view.

The primary view gives global scene context, while wrist cameras provide close-range evidence during occlusion and contact. MaskVLA explicitly discourages primary-view shortcuts during fine-tuning.

01

Diagnose overfitting

Grad-CAM analysis shows baseline attention drifting toward local texture cues and primary-view bias.

02

Mask the shortcut

Primary-camera masking forces the policy to learn from all available visual streams.

03

Recover robustness

At inference time the mask is disabled, letting the model use full multi-view observations.

Results

Consistent gains in simulation and on ALOHA robots.

RoboTwin2.0 benchmark

MaskVLA achieves the best average performance across four bimanual manipulation tasks. In hard mode, it reaches 75.0% average success, a 20.0-point gain over OpenVLA-OFT.

  • Best performance on 6 of 8 simulation subtasks.
  • Large gains on position-generalization tasks such as Stack bowls three.
  • MaskVLA-main-10% is the strongest ablation setting, averaging 74.0% in hard mode.

Real-world ALOHA evaluation

On the Airbot Mobile ALOHA platform, MaskVLA improves over OpenVLA-OFT across all four real-world tasks, including 85.0% success on Feed carrot and Place orange and 70.0% on Adjust cups.
